# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People deciding whether to spend time with someone they have not met properly yet: a teammate for a hackathon, a co-founder, a colleague, a friend, a date. The first crowd is the 20,000 builders on day one of a hackathon, on their phones between talks, in a loud hall or on the bus home, scrolling through people they might team up with. They are curious and a little impatient: they want to know fast whether someone is worth a real conversation. A second audience watches on a laptop: judges and friends seeing the demo.

The job: upload your own chat history once, approve what your agent may say about you, then let your agent meet someone else's agent and read how the two of you would probably get on, backed by your own words.

## Product Purpose

Your AI agent understands you, and theirs understands them. They meet first and play out how the two of you would probably react: would you get along, would you make a deal, do your views agree, could they help you with what you are stuck on, would you enjoy a first date. You get a short brief, and every line is backed by your own words. It simulates; it never scores.

Success looks like two people who would click finding each other faster, and every claim either showing the quote it rests on or saying plainly that it has none.

## Brand Personality

Playful, social, bold. The voice is plain and short, warm and candid, and honest about what it does not know: the agents say "probably", never "will". It should feel like a lively room where two friends of yours are talking about you kindly, and you get to listen in.

References, in Ambar's words: a merge of Lofty Lab (loftylab.framer.website: photos cut into shapes, stickers inside sentences, tilted colour panels that stack) and Nudge (nudge-folio.framer.website: a speech bubble that types, sticky notes, floating cursors with name tags, three accents at full strength). Taken from them: the energy, the cut-out photos, the stickers and notes, the cursors and bubbles. Not taken: condensed display type, monospace labels, all-caps headlines.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI product UI: a grey page, rounded cards stacked in a column, cards inside cards, pill labels on top of boxes, lots of empty space, a sentence of filler under every heading. (The app's ground is off-white #fffdfa by Ambar's choice on 3 Nov 2026; what makes this look generic is the boxes, not the warmth of the ground.) Ambar's words on the first attempt: "super ai slop and emptish, just curved cards and bad design, not intuitive at all and a lot of bs". <!-- plain-words: allow (his words, quoted) -->
- The techy look: monospace, terminal styling, codes, hazard or hi-vis colours, condensed docket type, anything taken from tickets or back-office paperwork.
- The quiet editorial-magazine lane: cream ground, italic serif headlines, ruled columns, no pictures.
- Dating and matching apps: swipe stacks, percent matches, scores, flames, hearts on everything. Meet First never scores a person.
- The looks of Ambar's other products, Makelet and Rentably.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the meeting, don't describe it.** Two agents, each in its own colour, talking about two real people. If a screen needs a paragraph to explain itself, the design is wrong.
2. **Their own words are the hero.** A quote from someone's own notes is the most important thing on any screen, and a claim without a quote looks visibly different from one with a quote.
3. **One obvious next step.** Every screen has a single main thing to do, and it is in the first screen on a phone.
4. **Play in the moments, plain in the tasks.** Stickers, cut-out photos, typing bubbles and motion live in the welcome, the meeting and the brief; forms, switches and settings stay fast and familiar.
5. **Never a number about a person.** No score, rank, percentage or count is ever shown about someone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA for every text and control colour pair, including text on the accent colours. Touch targets of at least 44 px. Reduced motion is respected: typing and floating effects stop and content appears at once. Real phones first (the phone gate is on real devices), then a laptop for the demo. Plain words with no jargon. For adults, 18 and over.
