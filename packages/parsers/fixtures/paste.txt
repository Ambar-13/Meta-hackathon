I work best before nine in the morning.

My manager Tom likes long meetings, and I do not.
I ask for an agenda before I accept any invite.
If there is no agenda, I offer a written update instead.
Tom has started sending agendas, which I count as a win.
I still decline anything after four.

I keep a notebook for every project I lead.
The first page always says why the project exists.
When a project loses its first page, I stop it.
I would rather end a project early than drag it on.
Ending early feels like failing, and I do it anyway.

Feedback lands best with me in writing, a day later.
In the moment I get defensive and say less than I mean.
A day later I can read it twice and answer properly.
I give feedback the same way, and I tell people why.
Most people thank me for the delay.

I am happiest when I teach someone a skill I learned the hard way.
Last year I ran a weekly lunch session on spreadsheet formulas.
Eight people came every week for three months.
That mattered more to me than the promotion that followed.
I want to run it again next year.

I do not answer work messages after eight at night.
The shared inbox for my project with Tom is tom.lee@example.com.
I check it once a day, after lunch.
Anything urgent can wait for that.

I travel light and I plan trips around one long walk.
The walk decides the city, not the other way round.
I keep the same small bag for every trip.
I have never checked a bag.

When I disagree with someone senior, I ask a question first.
If the answer does not settle it, I say what I think, once.
After that I commit to the decision, even if I lost.
I expect the same from people who work with me.
